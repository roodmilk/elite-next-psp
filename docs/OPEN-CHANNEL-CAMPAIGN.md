# ELITE: NEXT — THE OPEN CHANNEL CAMPAIGN BIBLE

Status: playable 24-chapter campaign introduced in 2.5.0. This document expands the short first-flight chapter and is subordinate to the safety, UI and performance rules in `DESIGN-BIBLE.md`.

## Promise and length

The first flight is the prologue. The Open Channel then runs for 24 chapters across four acts, with travel, trade, survey, salvage, interception, quiet observation and four permanent decisions. A direct critical-path run is designed for roughly ten hours; exploration, contracts and optional character calls extend it. Every chapter has a stable destination saved with the commander. Every required action has a recovery route and no chapter depends on a randomly available contract.

The campaign is an original story told against established Elite history. Lave and the remains of GalCop, the Federation, Empire and Alliance, INRA's mycoid atrocity, independent pilots, Aegis and the uncertainty around Thargoid motives inform the world. Existing dialogue, novels and GalNet copy are reference material, never text to reproduce. Thargoids remain rare: the player encounters traces and one remote signal, not a disposable procession of alien enemies.

## Main cast

- **Kei Aven**, an Explorers Guild coordinator, speaks precisely because panic makes her over-explain. Her arc is learning that care cannot become control.
- **Ryn Vale**, a missing surveyor, is funny, observant and infuriatingly willing to vanish to protect others. Her arc is learning to trust a crew with danger.
- **Venn Calder**, Lave traffic controller and former GalCop archivist, hides grief beneath procedure.
- **Iona Marr**, a local law officer, believes evidence matters more than uniforms. She can become the coalition's auditor.
- **Oru Sen**, xenobiologist and field naturalist, teaches the player to observe before naming a threat.
- **Tamsin Reed**, convoy captain, represents working pilots rather than a superpower.
- **Nadi Quill** and **Pip**, salvage engineer and literal maintenance drone, bring humour and practical solutions.
- **Sable Rook**, pirate negotiator, is dangerous but keeps explicit bargains. Trust does not make her harmless.
- **Director Cassian Voss**, Pale Meridian executive, believes reliable routes justify private control. He is an antagonist who can stand down, testify or flee; the story never requires his death.

## Persistent decisions

Four choices build Public, Guild, Law and Independent trust. No choice removes the next chapter. Choices change later assistance, radio reports, prices and the epilogue.

1. **The Price of Silence:** publish the ledger, give it to the Guild, or lodge it with Iona.
2. **What We Carry Home:** broadcast Ryn's evidence now, verify it first, or reveal only the active hazard.
3. **No Easy Flag:** accept pilots from the public convoy network, Guild survey teams, or a legally supervised task force.
4. **Who Keeps the Light:** choose the relay's final custodian under a public inspection charter.

## Act I — A place to return to

### 01. Something Worth Carrying

Mara recovered a sealed receiver Ryn left at an agricultural port. The player carries it to Lave while Kei explains that Ryn missed three scheduled calls. A forged hazard notice appears on the route. The receiver contains pauses that do not match machine timing.

KEI: “Ryn never missed a call. She missed meals, permits and once an entire moon. Never a call.”

### 02. The Quiet Between Pings

Oru asks the player to compare the receiver with a living signal. A scan of local fauna shows that migration calls occupy the pauses between navigation bursts. Shooting is never an objective; approaching too fast only resets the observation.

ORU: “Silence is part of the message. People forget that when they own a transmitter.”

### 03. All Hands Accounted For

Tamsin's convoy followed a newly published safe route and lost a tender. The player searches its final port, recovers lifeboat telemetry and learns the route was moved after departure. Optional rescue of every pod improves Independent trust.

### 04. A Lawful Mistake

Iona stops the player because Pale Meridian has reported Ryn's receiver stolen. The player compares timestamps, submits to inspection or pays under protest. All paths preserve the evidence and expose that the report predates the alleged theft.

IONA: “A badge is not evidence. Mine included.”

### 05. The Last Useful Thing

Nadi locates Ryn's damaged relay tender. A scan recovers an unedited recording: Pale Meridian has amplified a biological navigation signal and sold access to the clear lanes. Pip identifies a route key hidden in a maintenance checklist.

### 06. The Price of Silence

Sable offers safe passage in return for the ledger. Kei wants publication; Iona wants an evidence chain; the Guild promises peer review. The player makes the first persistent choice. Sable honours a refusal but warns that public data also reaches pirates.

## Act II — History in the route

### 07. No Cargo Is Just Cargo

A false closure has stranded a clinic. Carry power cells, escort Tamsin, or clear the interference buoy. The settlement survives on every route; the method changes local confidence and later support.

### 08. Ghosts of GalCop

Venn opens an archive beneath Lave's old traffic network. The player scans abandoned beacons that recall GalCop's promise of trade, mutual defence and local independence, along with the corruption and exhaustion that ended it. The lesson is neither nostalgia nor contempt: institutions fail when their records cannot be challenged.

VENN: “The old seal meant the lane belonged to everyone. Near the end, it meant nobody knew who was responsible.”

### 09. The Mycoid Ledger

An INRA-era record describes a fungal weapon deployed against living Thargoid technology and the pilot who was denied a return. Pale Meridian wants the research because organic relays share superficial traits. The player secures the archive; the game never allows mycoid to become a craftable weapon.

### 10. Where the Giants Turn

Oru leads a quiet survey of enormous spaceborne organisms. Matching three calls reveals that Ryn embedded a shelter coordinate in their migration cadence. This chapter gives the radio and scenery room to breathe.

### 11. The Person on the Other End

The player reaches Ryn, repairs her shelter and disables an amplifier. Kei and Ryn's reunion is relieved, angry and affectionate. Ryn becomes a recurring contact rather than a reward object.

KEI: “Start with ‘I'm alive.’”

RYN: “I'm alive.”

KEI: “Good. The clever part can wait.”

### 12. What We Carry Home

At Lave, the crew decides how much evidence to release. Immediate publication helps settlements prepare but alerts Meridian. Verification strengthens later legal action but delays route warnings. A limited alert is safer and earns less public trust. The choice changes Act III support.

## Act III — Three powers and no easy flag

### 13. A Map for Everyone

Ryn asks the player to place copies in a neutral archive. Each copy carries uncertainty and provenance rather than claiming perfect safety. Meridian agents offer to buy it; refusal is sufficient and combat is optional.

### 14. Federal Measure

A Federal envoy offers patrol coverage and rapid logistics in exchange for operational access. A rescue exercise shows the benefit; surveillance language shows the cost. The player listens and records terms without choosing yet.

### 15. Imperial Courtesy

An Imperial representative offers long-term funding and personal accountability through patronage. Sable points out the debt hidden beneath courtesy. Again, the player hears the case without being forced into allegiance.

### 16. Alliance of Necessity

Alliance delegates offer a coalition with slower decisions and public minutes. Iona tests their audit trail. The mission shows that decentralisation spreads power and responsibility together.

### 17. The Coldest Signal

Pip detects a structured nonhuman echo beyond the relay. The player scans from a safe distance and is explicitly told not to fire. The contact never appears as a normal target, never drops loot and leaves before identification. Oru calls it evidence; the news calls it a Thargoid; the log preserves uncertainty.

### 18. No Easy Flag

The player chooses the coalition that will approach the primary relay. Previous trust produces visible helpers: civilian fuel tenders, Guild survey markers, lawful ceasefire calls or Sable's covert route. Every coalition supports a non-lethal completion.

## Act IV — The channel opens

### 19. The Black Flight

Meridian interceptors block a public lane. The objective is to break the blockade: disable, outmanoeuvre or draw ships away while Tamsin passes. Destruction counts but is never the only verb.

### 20. Aegis Echo

An old research buoy records the virtues and failures of cross-superpower xeno work: shared science, defence and rescue can save lives, while secrecy and escalation can magnify disaster. Recovering it unlocks a safer amplifier shutdown sequence.

### 21. The Open Channel

At the primary relay, the coalition disables three amplifiers and broadcasts the evidence. Progress checkpoints after each node. Voss argues that control created dependable routes; Ryn answers that dependence is not consent. Voss can stand down if evidence and Law trust are high, evacuate if public trust is high, or flee after the relay is disabled.

### 22. The Answering Dark

With the amplifiers silent, a distant call answers. The player records it without pursuit. The visual language nods toward Thargoid technology but stops short of certainty. This preserves the scale and mystery of Elite's aliens.

RYN: “It answered the empty space we left.”

ORU: “Then our first useful act was making room.”

### 23. Who Keeps the Light

The player ratifies a custodian and inspection charter. The losing groups retain access to the public route data. Trust changes the tone and benefits, never the availability of fuel, repair or the postgame.

### 24. A Berth Kept Warm

Return to Lave. Venn assigns berth six permanently. Kei admits that asking the player to bring Ryn home also gave the player a home. The credits show the player's four choices and surviving relationships, then offer a research beacon, relief route or salvage tug as the first post-story project.

## Production and validation rules

- Bind a chapter destination once, save it, and route to it with the real jump graph.
- Critical objectives use authored state, never the current random board inventory.
- Rewards commit with chapter advancement and cannot repeat after loading.
- A scan, kill or arrival made before accepting a chapter cannot complete it.
- Essential evidence uses flags, not ordinary cargo capacity.
- Choice text states who receives authority and what oversight applies.
- Dialogue uses a named blue speaker panel; player replies use orange right-tailed panels.
- The HUD shows only the immediate verb and destination. Full context stays in Mission Log.
- Suspend, death and leaving a system preserve the chapter and its destination.
