ELITE: NEXT — IMMERSIVE SPACE EXPANSION
Consolidated design and implementation handoff — 7 October 2026

STATUS AND INTENT
This document records the user's requested direction. It is a proposed feature
specification, not evidence that these features are already implemented.
Main development assistant: inspect the current code first, preserve existing
work, and integrate these features with current controls, missions and saves.
Do not replace working systems unnecessarily or claim completion from prose.

The goal is expansive, beautiful, inhabited space on PSP. Most new discoveries
are visually interesting objects with a name, a short contact response and a
small animation. They do NOT each need a new gameplay loop, interior or mission.
The user specifically prefers seeing, discovering, briefly interacting and
moving on over making every location deep or complicated.

1. PLATFORM AND VISUAL DIRECTION

Design for native PSP 480x272. Use readable silhouettes, strong compositions,
restrained neon, warm windows, dark space and the established retro pixel-art
direction. Signs, icons and portraits must be drawn for their displayed size.
Do not create detailed large artwork and shrink it until it becomes unreadable.
Keep central flight views clear; routine information belongs in HUD bands.

Use depth and motion to sell scale: distant stars barely move, nearby particles
pass the ship, destinations grow gradually and traffic becomes clearer nearby.
Dust and ice should cluster around appropriate regions, not cover every system.
Vary lighting near suns, behind planets and inside rings. Restrained glints,
exhaust glow and lens flare should support visibility rather than obscure it.
Avoid constant shaking, flashing and excessive bloom.

2. BIGGER, MORE VARIED SOLAR SYSTEMS

Make systems substantially larger. Planets and stations should be much farther
apart and occupy varied locations, bearings and elevations. Use deterministic
seeded generation so returning players recognise the same system.

Place stations according to purpose: near planets, resources, shipping routes
or remote facilities. Do not make every system a similar forward-facing skyline.
Create clusters of activity separated by quiet expanses. Scatter discoveries
along and away from travel routes; avoid evenly spaced attractions everywhere.

Wealth, population, danger and purpose are separate influences. A small rich
station may be busy and polished; a huge poor station may have closed berths.
Poor systems can be colourful, welcoming and cared for. Not all are ruins.

Scale distance against actual travel time. Sustained boost must make journeys
practical. Keep steering, speed readouts, braking and approach warnings reliable.
Maps, targeting, mission destinations and arrival distances must agree.

3. LIGHTWEIGHT DISCOVERY INTERACTIONS

Every contactable discovery should have a distinctive name, visual identity,
short response and appropriate speaker. Use current targeting/contact controls.
Use X to confirm/advance where appropriate, without conflicting with firing.
Do not invent a second inconsistent control scheme.

Staffed object: short greeting. Automated object: recorded message/status.
Abandoned object: ship-computer observation. Animal: scan observation, no speech.
Optional tiny response: lamp blink, advertisement change, dish turn or signal.
Usually one or two sentences, then close and continue flying.
Optional Codex recording should be unobtrusive. Do not turn every visit into
a reward popup or automatically create a mission.

8. STATIONS THAT FEEL ALIVE

Traffic depends on wealth, function and activity, not only station size.
Rich normal-sized stations should have this too, not just mega-capital hubs.

Shuttle ports: small craft approach on varied curved routes, slow down, form
short irregular queues, align with the port, enter and disappear inside.
After varied service intervals, ships emerge, clear the hull and accelerate away.
Ports operate independently, never in obvious synchronised loops.

Service berths: parked ships and occasional maintenance craft.
Freight pads: enormous freighters land externally and stay for meaningful periods.
Luxury berths: yachts and executive shuttles; industrial berths: haulers and tugs.
Some ports remain empty; some closed. Busy need not mean constant chaos.

Routes follow station rotation and avoid the structure, neighbouring ports and
other traffic. Preserve the player's docking entrance AND full approach/exit
corridor. Decorative ships must never block or damage the player unexpectedly.

Sell distance with silhouettes and navigation lights. Add hull detail nearby.
Use bounded traffic pools rather than treating every decorative ship as a full
combat NPC. They may initially remain non-targetable scenery; do not imply
combat functionality they do not possess.

Where practical, preserve continuity through windows: a ship seen landing
outside remains on its pad when viewed from the bar. Keep traffic transforms
and timestamps consistent between exterior and interior views.

9. EXTERNAL FREIGHTER LANDING SPECTACLE

Large stations, including wealthy non-capital stations, can have external pads.

Cycle: wide slow approach -> match pad motion -> manoeuvring thruster pulses ->
settle onto landing gear/clamps -> brief vent clouds -> unloading activity ->
release clamps -> lift clear -> accelerate away.

Venting in vacuum should expand and fade, not rise like atmospheric smoke.
Thruster exhaust briefly lights the pad. Cargo lamps and small service vehicles
provide restrained activity while the freighter is parked.
From far away emphasise scale, pad markings, silhouette and engine flashes.
Nearby reveal clamps and cargo. Giant arrivals should be occasional events.

10. MOVING LUXURY CRUISE FREIGHTERS

These travel throughout the galaxy; they are not exclusive to capital systems.
Rich systems get more; remote systems may occasionally receive older liners.

Give each a name, operator, distinct hull, warm passenger windows, observation
domes and fictional billboard panels. Advertisements are large simple pixel-art
graphics with very short readable copy, not tiny paragraphs.
Bright engine cores and tasteful fiery-looking exhaust trails sell their motion.

Within a system, follow scenic circuits visiting several planets. Use smooth
arcs: travel to a world -> broad scenic pass outside atmosphere/rings -> slow
near an attraction -> continue to the next stop -> eventually depart the system.
Do not use sharp waypoint turns or blindly cross planetary bodies.
Routes may be an inner-world express, grand planetary tour or outer-ring cruise.

Players can fly alongside and contact the liner for a greeting and next stop.
Shuttles may operate from dedicated ports. Boarding is a separate optional
future feature; do not block exterior implementation on new interiors.
If boarding is implemented, moving docking paths and window views must remain
consistent with the ship's actual motion.

10A. SHARED LARGE-SHIP TECHNOLOGY AND PERFORMANCE

Freighters and cruise liners should share the same large-vessel foundation:
route following, smooth turning, distance-based detail, lighting, engine effects,
traffic ports and any implemented docking/berthing behaviour. Give them separate
visual profiles and appropriate movement tuning rather than duplicate engines.

Big freighters must be substantially larger and more detailed than their current
presentation. Flying alongside should reveal their enormous length and bulk.
Use cargo spines, container banks, structural ribs, engine clusters, recessed
bays, navigation lights and clearly scaled windows/service craft to sell size.
Cruise liners use passenger decks, panoramic windows, observation structures,
advertisement panels and leisure-oriented silhouettes on the shared foundation.
Do not simply recolour one identical hull for both roles.

FPS IS PARAMOUNT. More size and visual detail must not cause stutter or unstable
frame times. Measure against the existing physical-PSP frame budget and baseline.
Keep geometry and effects bounded; cull off-screen detail and use distance-based
levels of detail. Distant hulls need silhouettes and a few lights. Reveal selected
geometry, surface panels and restrained exhaust detail nearby. Prefer reusable
surface detail over modelling every small feature. Limit active huge ships and
nearby shuttle effects; stagger expensive updates. Avoid per-frame allocation.

Use visual hull dimensions consistently for collision, targeting, docking and
routes. Expanding a freighter must not leave old small collision bounds, obscure
player ports or create unsafe planetary passes. Detail transitions should avoid
obvious popping. Test close flybys, multiple visible capital hulls, busy stations
and effects-heavy travel on physical PSP; PPSSPP alone cannot certify FPS.
If a visual improvement exceeds the budget, reduce its cost before shipping.
11. HUGE FLY-THROUGH PLANETARY RINGS

Rings extend far beyond planets and are navigable in normal spaceflight.
Flying into rings MUST NOT automatically trigger EVA or planetary landing.
Only the existing appropriate planetary approach region may offer landing.

Far away: broad luminous bands, gaps and a strong planetary silhouette.
Approaching: separate lanes, dark gaps, dust and ice.
Inside: small particles, larger rocks, open channels and occasional huge chunks.
Vary ring tilt, width, density, palette and composition per planet.

Larger asteroids are actual shootable mineable objects using existing damage,
ore and collection systems. Decorative grains are not all collision objects.
Rendering, mineable placement and collision share the same world-space ring
geometry. Do not render a camera-facing painted ellipse that disagrees with
where the player can fly and find rocks.
Do not repopulate destroyed rocks immediately just because the player turns
away. Use bounded local detail without obvious popping or endless reward exploits.

12. HYPERSPACE CHARGE AND CONTINUOUS FIRST-PERSON EFFECT

Retain selecting a destination system and boosting toward its departure heading.
Improve the existing blue hyperdrive charge meter and bottom charging text.

0-50%: normal view, steadily building engine sound and subtle edge shimmer.
50-85%: charge noticeably faster; mega warp streaks lengthen and intensify.
The actual departure scene stretches outward around a central vanishing point.
Add gentle distortion ripples and restrained edge colour separation.
85-100%: rapid final charge, peak streaks and restrained space-view vibration.
Keep meter and essential HUD stable, readable and undistorted.

Initial tuning proposal: approximately five seconds total, with three seconds
for the first half and two for the accelerating second half. This is a starting
point for testing, not a user-mandated exact duration.

At entry: stretch/dissolve the real departure view into luminous trails.
Conceal the scene transition at peak distortion; avoid obvious hard cuts.
At arrival: show the actual destination scene already visible but distorted.
Unwind distortion so its planets, stars and stations resolve smoothly into place.
Shorten trails, remove colour separation and settle vibration to a stable view.
Finish with a concise arrival notification.

The first-person space view remains the visual foundation throughout. Do not
replace it with a disconnected generic warp tunnel or black loading screen.

Potential PSP technique: low-resolution flight-view captures drawn through a
coarse textured mesh; blend departure/destination during maximum distortion;
return seamlessly to live rendering. Inspect existing renderer before choosing
the implementation. Bound memory and verify real-hardware performance.

Visual distortion must never alter world coordinates, steering or collision.
Exaggerate speed visually without causing uncontrolled physical acceleration
near objects. Charge remains monotonic while eligible. Validate fuel and route
before the jump; execute travel once. Existing cancellation rules fade effects
out smoothly. Keep ordinary boost visually distinct from hyperspace.

13. OUTER-SYSTEM ARRIVAL AND FUEL

Arrive far from stations so the player journeys into the system. Choose a safe
outer region based on incoming route, with modest variation between visits.
Avoid planets, rings, station hulls, freight routes and hazardous structures.

As distortion clears, reveal distant destinations and signals. Keep the tracked
destination available and make distance/navigation clear. Nearby discoveries
may invite a detour, but do not spawn the same welcome cluster every time.

Fuel policy to integrate deliberately with current code:
- Ordinary flight should not strand the ship by consuming jump fuel.
- Boost uses heat rather than unexpectedly draining escape fuel.
- Hyperspace consumes the amount shown before committing.
- Ice-rich asteroid fuel recovery is a NEW feature if not already implemented:
  clearly marked ice resources plus a fuel-processing capability.
- Include a dependable emergency recovery such as a callable fuel tender.
  Do not make recovery depend on owning equipment the stranded player lacks.

Do not silently change fuel rules without updating UI, tests and documentation.
Space should feel vast without creating unwinnable saves or excessive waiting.

14. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER AND CHECKS

Suggested order, adaptable to the existing code:
1 Audit distances, ring geometry, flight/boost, world seeds and existing POIs.
2 Shared world-space rings and shootable asteroids with safe approach boundaries.
3 A few discovery prototypes: listening post, wreck, animal cage, billboard.
4 A reusable named discovery/contact renderer and bounded placement system.
5 Independent station traffic ports and external freight pads.
6 Moving cruise liner circuits with varied advertisements.
7 Expanded distance tuning, safe outer arrivals and hyperspace transitions.
8 Broaden the scenery catalogue after the initial locations pass testing.

Avoid expanding every system with untested changes at once. Certify a Lave
slice and representative poor/rich/capital systems, then apply seeded variety.

Checks must cover stable placement, valid targeting/contact, no UI clipping,
ring visual/physical agreement, shootable rocks and actual ore collection,
no ring-triggered landing, traffic clear of player docking, moving-port safety,
no collisions with hidden decorative objects, safe warp arrivals, fuel recovery,
jump cancellation, no duplicate jumps, restored effects after travel, save/load,
and performance/memory on physical PSP. Preserve current story/tutorial routes.

Final aim: large journeys, memorable sights, brief personality-filled contacts,
working-looking stations and beautiful continuous travel, without turning every
piece of scenery into a complicated new subsystem.
