# ELITE: NEXT — A+++ visual pass

**Status:** active art-direction execution plan  
**Reference language:** original 1950s–70s science-fiction illustration, paperback covers and practical miniature photography  
**Runtime constraint:** PSP 480×272; proposed boards are not runtime art until baked and tested

This is the quality bar for taking ELITE: NEXT from a functional prototype to a memorable, authored game. It uses the supplied reference art as broad visual research—not as material to copy.

## What A+++ means

The player should recognize the world before reading the labels:

- a strong composition with one hero object;
- a clear foreground/middle/background depth stack;
- saturated but controlled colour;
- a believable practical light source;
- one strange or narrative detail that creates curiosity;
- readable interaction and objective hierarchy;
- no clipped text, accidental overlaps or competing decoration;
- a result that still works at 1× PSP resolution.

More pixels alone will not reach this bar. Every screen needs better staging.

## Stage 1 — composition surgery

**Action:** redesign each major screen around a focal hierarchy.

### Rules

1. Pick one hero: station aperture, planet limb, terminal, character or ship.
2. Put it on a one-third intersection, never under the primary HUD target.
3. Reserve negative space for gameplay text before adding decoration.
4. Use three depth planes: atmospheric/background, structure/props, focal/NPC.
5. Use one key light and one signal light. Do not make every panel glow.
6. Make one story detail visible: a departing freighter, warning flag, survey specimen, damaged dome or unfamiliar beacon.

### Current station problem

The current station screen presents many tabs, frames, labels and characters with equal weight. The A+++ correction is a **room first, interface second** composition: the room establishes place, the selected interaction gets one deliberate highlight, and the footer explains the action.

## Stage 2 — station room redesign

Apply the room kit to each runtime room:

- **HALL:** long vanishing corridor, one destination hatch, quiet negative space.
- **HUB:** large berth window/arrival board, tiny traffic silhouettes, Venn as scale.
- **CHANDLERY:** warm counter and one selected part; shelves recede into shadow.
- **BAR:** counter light, two silhouettes, one rumour detail; no neon wall clutter.
- **CARGO BAY:** cargo lane, lift, manifest light and one marked crate.
- **CLINIC:** cool task lamp, treatment bench, one medic silhouette.
- **GUILD:** evidence wall, survey terminal and warm desk pool around Kei.
- **CUSTOMS:** scanner gate, officer silhouette and a single red inspection cue.

### Acceptance gate

At 1×, a reviewer can name the room, focal object and available action in one second without reading every label.

## Stage 3 — planet and deep-space identity

### Planets

- Preserve six seeded identity families: ocean/temperate, red dune, ice, fungal, copper badlands and airless ruin.
- Give each one a large colour/value read, a distinct limb or cloud gesture, and one small landmark cue.
- Use atmospheric perspective: distant terrain is lower contrast, not more detailed.
- Keep rings, moons and clouds sparse enough to preserve the body silhouette.

### Space

- Use three star-depth bands, not a uniform starfield.
- Frame the hero body with one relay, belt, moon or traffic element.
- Give nebulae an asymmetrical painted mass and a quiet side for HUD copy.
- Use engine wakes, beacons and lens ghosts as authored accents, never constant wallpaper.

### Acceptance gate

Hide the labels. If the reviewer cannot distinguish the body family and scene mood from the silhouette and colour alone, the pass is not finished.

## Stage 4 — character and creature storytelling

- Use adult, graphic silhouettes with strong coats, helmets, tools and posture.
- Give each faction a shape language before colour: trader cargo wear, law structure, explorer survey gear, pirate salvage.
- Reserve expressive alien life for authored moments, Codex discoveries and rare signals.
- Stage NPCs in relation to props: looking at a board, guarding a hatch, repairing a loader or carrying a specimen.
- Never fill a scene with anonymous bodies just to make it feel busy.

## Stage 5 — interface reduction and polish

- Remove borders that do not separate information.
- Replace repeated rectangular frames with spacing, alignment and one selected edge.
- Keep cyan for navigation/safe route, amber for focus/objective, red for danger.
- Use one type scale per hierarchy level.
- Keep portraits, names and dialogue inside the existing safe bands.
- Make selected state readable through fill + edge + shape, not colour alone.
- Audit every label for clipping at native resolution.

The screenshot supplied by the commander is the baseline audit: it has promising cyan/amber identity but too many equal-weight bars, frames and floating labels. The pass must simplify before it adds detail.

## Stage 6 — bake and integration gates

Every proposed asset or room must ship with:

- source board and provenance;
- native 480×272 review image;
- palette and dimensions;
- reduced-effects/high-contrast check;
- memory and draw-list estimate;
- frame-time result in the densest scene;
- current/proposed label in the review capture.

No generated concept becomes runtime art automatically. It becomes a target for an original, hand-authored, native-size asset.

## Execution order

1. Redesign one representative station room: **HUB / ARRIVALS**.
2. Review it at 1× and remove every nonessential border.
3. Apply the same composition grammar to CHANDLERY and GUILD.
4. Create one cinematic planet approach and one quiet deep-space composition.
5. Compare current and proposed captures side-by-side.
6. Hand the station lane the approved focal object, palette, light key and cut order.
7. Repeat only after the first three screens meet the one-second-read gate.

The goal is a small set of excellent screens that establish the identity everywhere, not a large quantity of inconsistent art.
